---
title: What did a county magistrate do?
description: The magistrate was often the most important regular state official people encountered, but relied heavily on clerks and local intermediaries.
publishedAt: 2026-10-02
section: Government
---

A county magistrate headed the county office, or *yamen*. In late imperial China, this office dealt with taxes, order, disputes, public works, grain, documents, and communication with higher levels of government.

This made the magistrate a judge, administrator, tax official, police authority, and representative of the emperor’s government all at once.

## One official, many tasks

The office was not run by the magistrate alone. Clerks, runners, village leaders, brokers, gentry, and other local people supplied information and carried out routine work. Their knowledge was indispensable—and could also create opportunities for delay, manipulation, or corruption.

A magistrate’s workload could be immense. The state’s formal bureaucracy was thin relative to the population, so local governance depended on both official authority and unofficial collaboration.

## What this means for sources

A court case or tax record may show the magistrate’s seal, but the voices and actions behind it can include many other people. “The state” was not a single person at the county level.
