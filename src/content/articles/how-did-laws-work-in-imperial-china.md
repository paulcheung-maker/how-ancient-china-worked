## The short answer

Imperial China had detailed legal codes, but law did not work through a modern system of independent judges, lawyers, and equal courtroom access. Local magistrates handled many cases; families and communities often tried mediation first; and serious punishments were reviewed through higher levels of government.

Law was one part of governing alongside family authority, local custom, moral teaching, and administrative practice.

## Legal codes and administration

Dynasties issued codes that defined crimes, penalties, procedures, and official responsibilities. The Tang Code became especially influential for later legal traditions. Codes distinguished offenses by circumstances and, in some cases, by status and family relationship.

Officials were expected to know the law, though real enforcement depended on staff, records, witnesses, transport, local power, and political pressure.

## The magistrate's court

At county level, a magistrate could investigate disputes, hear accusations, collect evidence, punish certain offenses, and report or transfer serious cases. He was administrator, judge, tax official, and representative of the state at the same time.

That concentration of roles made the county office important but also limited. One magistrate and a small staff governed many people, so not every dispute reached a formal hearing.

## Mediation and family authority

Many conflicts over marriage, land, debt, inheritance, or behavior were handled within families, lineages, guilds, villages, or local networks. Mediation could be quicker and less costly than going to court, but it could also favor powerful relatives or local elites.

People did use the courts, especially when informal settlement failed. The idea that ordinary people never had legal recourse is too simple.

## Punishment and review

Penalties ranged from fines and beatings to penal servitude, exile, and execution. The most serious cases could be reviewed at higher administrative levels, particularly when the death penalty was involved. Procedures differed by dynasty and did not eliminate abuse or error.
