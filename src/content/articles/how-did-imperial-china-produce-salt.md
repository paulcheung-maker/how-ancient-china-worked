## The short answer

Imperial China produced salt from seawater, salt lakes, and underground brine wells. Because everyone needed salt and it was relatively easy to tax, governments often tried to control its production and sale.

## Different environments, different methods

Coastal producers evaporated seawater. Inland regions drew brine from wells or used salt lakes. Techniques, labor systems, and environmental costs varied greatly by place.

## Why governments cared

Salt revenue could fund armies and administration. Monopoly systems promised income but also created incentives for smuggling, corruption, high prices, and local resistance. Salt policy shows how an everyday necessity connected household life with state power.
