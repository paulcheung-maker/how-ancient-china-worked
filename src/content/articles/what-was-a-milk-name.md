title: What was a milk name in imperial China?
description: A childhood name could coexist with a personal name, courtesy name, and later appellations.

A “milk name” is one translation for *ruming* (乳名): a childhood or intimate name used within a family. It is often also described as a baby name or childhood name.

It should not be confused with a personal name (*ming*) or an adult courtesy name (*zi*). A person could have more than one name during a lifetime, with each used in a different relationship or setting.

## Why use a separate childhood name?

Family names could be affectionate, practical, protective, or deliberately ordinary. The surviving evidence is uneven, so it is risky to invent a universal rule about how every household chose one. What matters is that a childhood name belonged to a more intimate sphere than the names used in formal documents and adult social life.

Some sources record a childhood name; many do not. The absence of a *ruming* in a genealogy or biography does not prove that none existed.

## How should writers use it?

For historical fiction, a milk name can help distinguish home life from public life—but it should fit the period, region, and social setting. Avoid treating it as a modern legal first name or assuming that it was always secret.

For historical research, preserve the source’s terminology. If the source calls a name *ruming*, translate it as “childhood name” or “milk name” and distinguish it from *ming*, *zi*, and *hao*.
