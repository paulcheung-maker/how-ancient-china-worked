## The short answer

Canals connected farming regions, cities, markets, and capitals. They moved grain, taxes, officials, troops, and commercial goods more efficiently than many overland routes, making waterways central to both government and trade.

## The Grand Canal

The Grand Canal linked northern and southern regions through a system built and rebuilt over centuries. It helped supply northern political centers with grain from productive southern areas.

## Benefits and limits

Water transport could lower costs, but canals required dredging, locks, labor, security, and constant repair. Floods, war, silting, and political neglect could interrupt them. Infrastructure was powerful, but never automatic.
