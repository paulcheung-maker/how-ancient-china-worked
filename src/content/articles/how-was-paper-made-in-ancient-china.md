## The short answer

Early Chinese papermakers processed plant fibers, bark, rags, and other materials into pulp, spread it on a screen, pressed away water, and dried the resulting sheet. Methods and materials changed across time and region.

Paper was lighter and usually cheaper than bamboo slips or silk. That made records, letters, books, education, and government administration more practical.

## Not one inventor or one recipe

Cai Lun is traditionally associated with improvements under the Eastern Han, but archaeological finds show paper existed earlier. Papermaking was a craft developed through many workers and workshops, not a single sudden invention.

## Why it mattered

A cheaper writing surface supported copying, archives, religious texts, commerce, and later printing. Paper also travelled beyond China through long-distance contact, though the details of transfer differed by region.
