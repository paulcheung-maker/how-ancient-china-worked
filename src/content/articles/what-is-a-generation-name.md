A generation name is a character shared by people of the same generation in a family or lineage. It is often called a **generation character** or **generation marker**.

In a common pattern, it came after the surname and before the individually chosen part of the personal name. Male patrilineal cousins might therefore share one character while having different final characters.

## Where did the character come from?

Many lineages preserved a sequence of generation characters in a genealogy. The sequence could form a poem or moral statement, with each generation assigned the next character. It helped relatives place one another within a descent line.

Not every family used the practice, and the practice was not uniform. Some families marked generation through a shared written radical rather than an identical character. Migration, lost genealogies, social change, and family choice could interrupt the sequence.

## Did women use generation names?

Sometimes, but less uniformly. A woman might share the generation character used by brothers, use a different female sequence, or have no documented generation name. Surviving sources are often less precise about women’s personal names, so absence in a record should not be overinterpreted.

## What it does not prove

A shared character does not automatically prove that two people were close relatives. It may point to a lineage practice, but identification requires the relevant genealogy, place, and period. Nor is the second character of every three-character name a generation marker.

When reading a family history, treat a generation name as a useful clue to kinship structure—not as a complete family tree.
